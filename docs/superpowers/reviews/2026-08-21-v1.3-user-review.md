# v1.3 user review — 2026-08-20 (drives M12)

User feedback after the v1.3 live review (verbatim themes):

1. **Feeding is not that good** — the feeding beats still don't read well despite M11's mote and
   glyph work; needs a fresh look at what's actually unconvincing (pacing? the mote itself? clip
   readability?) rather than assuming M11's specific mechanism was the whole fix.
2. **Kangaroo/joey behavior is not accurate** — babies appearing to go toward/into the pouch
   rather than just sitting beside the mother, or visually conflicting with the decorative joey
   art that doesn't actually carry them.
3. **Animal rigs should be more realistic and better looking and recognizable** — general ask
   spanning multiple/all 12 species; needs scoping (which species first, what "recognizable"
   means) before a plan is written.

Meta: M12 opened for investigation; v2 Caretaker World stays next-after-that (only once the user
decides to proceed; feeding, care, unlocking families).

## Root causes (from 2026-08-21 brainstorm)

- **Feeding legibility gap (real design issue):** nothing in the codebase posed one creature
  relative to another (mother and baby stood side-by-side at arbitrary distance) and `FEED_RANGE`
  was ~90 units (about a body length). A stationary nursing mother's `heading` was never updated
  so she often faced away from the baby she fed, eroding the "moment you can watch." Carrying
  only works on-contact when both actors are in one place — the `feedContactRing` mechanism.
- **Kangaroo joey speed incompatibility (real mechanical problem):** a joey's base speed is 4.95
  u/tick while its mother's is 9 u/tick — it could never keep pace when hopping together. Crossing
  the old 140-unit leash took ~1.6 seconds, visibly separating them. A pouch-carry system (the
  joey mounted to the mother's rig, position-derived from carrier, never its own creature
  behavior) is the real solution.
- **Rig silhouette/form/motion clarity (design observation):** the four species touched in M11/M12
  (rabbit, deer, robin, kangaroo) revealed a pattern: rigs that read as themselves share clear
  silhouette first, then markings (distinct regions of color, not flat fills), shading (layered
  alpha for depth), and motion (gait cycles matched to real creatures' speed). The remaining
  eight species lack this deliberate pass.

## Decisions (controller, user rulings from brainstorm)

- **Feeding legibility (Thread A):** fix via contact ring (mutual distance < 40 units), mutual
  facing (mother turns to look at baby via `turnToward` behavior), four-beat pacing (30-tick
  settle → 90-tick nursing/carry → 40-tick linger → release), longer/head-anchored mote bursts
  (3-mote staggered burst, 900ms lifetime), and beat-aware inspect-card text so every feeding is
  a moment you can watch. User explicitly did NOT want a discovery/marker system — the existing
  behavior was to be made legible, not replaced.
- **Real kangaroo pouch-carry (Thread B):** build as a real sim system (`Creature.carriedBy`),
  not decorative art. The joey mounts to the mother (zero-draw transitions deterministic to the
  joey's idHash), rides in the rig's `pouch` container (position-derived-from-carrier rendering
  via Pixi reparenting), and dismounts on a graze window (idHash-phased interval). The decorative
  joey part is deleted and superseded by the real baby's reparented view. This was an explicit,
  justified reversal of the M10/M11 decision to avoid it as "a new system" — the speed mismatch
  is real and the user accepted the cost.
- **Rig-art recipe (Thread C):** write a reviewed, checkable recipe (silhouette/markings/shading/
  motion rules as four dimensions); apply it to four species first (rabbit, deer, robin, kangaroo)
  to prove it works; defer the remaining eight species to M13. User chose this scope deliberately
  over either a 12-species sweep (too much risk/time for one task) or splitting art into its own
  milestone — a few done *well* and reviewable, not rushed.
